## The one-sentence version

The <Term id="internet">internet</Term> is millions of <Term id="network">networks</Term> joined together, so a message can travel from any one of them to any other.

If a network is one street of houses passing notes, the internet is every street in the world, joined up by roads.

## Why it matters in security

The internet joins your network to everyone else's, including people you'd never let in the door. That's why organisations put guards where their network meets the internet, and why attackers from anywhere in the world can knock on a computer that's reachable from it.

It's also why this app is careful. Scanning or testing a real computer on the internet without its owner's permission is a crime. So nothing in Hacker Simulation ever touches the real internet.

## How it actually works

Every home and office network has a <Term id="router">router</Term>: the box that joins it to the roads outside. When a message is for a computer on your own street, it stays at home. When it's for somewhere else, the router hands it on, and routers all over the world pass it along until it arrives.

Some addresses are kept aside for private networks and can never be reached across the internet: every address starting `10.` or `192.168.` is one of them. Names ending in `.example` are kept aside too, and never lead to a real website. Every network, address and name in this app uses those.

## Common misconceptions

- **"The internet and the web are the same thing."** The web, with its websites and browsers, is one of many things that travel over the internet. Email and video calls use it too.
- **"Anything I can reach on the internet is public, so I may test it."** Being able to reach a computer isn't permission to test it. Its owner decides.
- **"Private addresses are secret."** They're not hidden, they're only local: millions of homes use `192.168.` addresses on their own street, and none of them can be reached from outside.
